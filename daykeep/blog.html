Daykeep is nearly here

Daykeep is in final testing and heading to the App Store. It is a calm place to keep your days: write an entry, drop in the photos that belong with it, and keep as many journals as you like. It runs on iPhone, iPad and Mac as a single purchase, and syncs privately through your own iCloud — there is no Daykeep account and no server of mine that ever sees your writing.

The app is free to use. An optional one-off Daykeep Plus unlock adds unlimited journals, clean un-marked printing, and the full tools for restoring backups. No subscription.

Journal here, print there — Daykeep meets Mosaic Collage Maker

The feature I am most pleased with connects Daykeep to my other app, Mosaic Collage Maker. You can export a whole journal — photos, captions and the writing between them — as one file, open it in Collage on your Mac or PC, and watch it pour into an editable, print-ready photobook.

You pick a style, tidy anything you like, add a cover, and export a print-ready PDF to order from any printer. The export from Daykeep is free; Collage is the part that does the design. It turns a year of journalling into a book you can hold.

Why another journal app?

I wanted a journal that treated my photos as part of the story rather than an afterthought, kept my writing genuinely private, and let me get my words back out again — as a file, or as a printed book — whenever I wanted. I could not find one that did all three without a subscription, so I built it.

The principles are simple: your journal is yours, it lives in your own iCloud, and Daykeep never holds it hostage. Restoring your own backup is always free. More notes will follow here as launch approaches.
