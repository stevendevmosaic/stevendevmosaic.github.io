What it does

  • One-click buttons. Open a folder, file, app or website. Run any of your Shortcuts. Press a key combination or paste a piece of text. Run a shell command or an AppleScript.
  • Stacks. Group buttons into a stack that flies out upwards (or downwards, if the bar is at the top of the screen).
  • Folder buttons. Click a folder to browse what is inside it, right from the bar, and step into sub-folders.
  • Buttons that follow the app you are using. Add a set of buttons for Mail, Safari, Final Cut Pro or anything else. They appear after a divider only while that app is in front.
  • Ready-made sets. One click adds a set for Mail, Safari, Finder, Terminal, Messages, Photos, Excel, Word, Zoom, Teams, Final Cut Pro or Music, and one click removes it again.
  • Easy to arrange. Drag buttons to reorder them, drag them into and out of stacks, and drop an app, folder, file or web link on the bar to make a button. Drop a file on an app button to open it with that app.
  • Touch Bar-style controls. Add a volume slider, a brightness slider, play/pause, next and previous track, mute, buttons for Music and Spotify, and an emoji picker, each in one click from the right-click menu.
  • Now Playing. A button that shows the track, artist and cover art from Apple Music, Spotify, Qobuz or a Sonos speaker on your network, and plays or pauses with a tap.
  • Stock prices. A button for any share or index, with the day’s change and a small chart. Quotes are delayed, and a tap opens the stock in Yahoo Finance.
  • Compact or Normal. Every button shares one height, and you can switch the whole bar to a larger Normal size from the right-click menu.
  • Share a setup. Export your whole layout, or just one stack or one app's set, and import other people's. Your licence is never included, and you can remove personal details before sharing.
  • Stays out of the way. Auto-hide, a hotkey to show and hide it, a menu bar icon, and it remembers where you put it.
  • Safe to experiment. Undo your last changes, lock buttons so they cannot be moved or removed, and automatic backups you can restore from.

Permissions it may ask for

BarDeck asks only when you use a feature that needs it, and everything else works without them.

  • Accessibility, for the press keys and paste text buttons, which work by sending keystrokes to the app in front.
  • Automation, for the New Folder button, which asks Finder to create a folder in the window you have open, and for the Apple Music and Spotify buttons, which ask to read and control those apps.
  • Accessibility is also used by the Qobuz Now Playing button, which reads the track shown in the Qobuz window.
  • Local network, for the Sonos Now Playing button, which looks for speakers on your network. Stock buttons ask Yahoo Finance for delayed quotes over the internet.

Questions

Why isn’t it on the Mac App Store?
Apps in the Mac App Store have to run in a sandbox, and the sandbox does not allow an app to send keystrokes to other apps or to script Finder. Those are some of the most useful things a button can do, so BarDeck is sold directly instead.
Does it replace the Dock?
No. It sits alongside it. Think of it as a set of your own shortcuts that is always in reach, not a launcher for everything.
What happens if I move to a new Mac?
Install BarDeck there and enter the same licence key. Your buttons live in a small file you can copy across (the right-click menu has Edit config file and Backups).
Do I have to pay for updates?
No. Your licence covers every 1.x version, including new features as well as fixes. If a 2.0 ever arrives it may be a paid upgrade, and I would say so clearly before you were asked to pay anything. 1.x would keep working either way.
Does it update itself?
Yes. It checks for new versions in the background and asks before installing anything. You can also choose Check for Updates… from its menu.
