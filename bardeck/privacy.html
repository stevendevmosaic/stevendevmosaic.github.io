⌂ Apps BarDeck Privacy Support

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 24 October 2026

BarDeck keeps your buttons and settings on your own Mac. There is no account, no analytics and no tracking, and I, the developer, do not collect information about you or how you use the app.

Who this is from

BarDeck is made by Steven Muir, an independent developer.

What stays on your Mac

  • Your buttons, stacks and settings are saved in a file in your home folder (~/.config/bardeck), with automatic backups beside it.
  • Your licence key and the date your trial began are stored in your Mac’s keychain. The key is checked on your Mac; it is not sent anywhere.
  • The bar’s position and a few preferences are saved in the app’s settings.

What the app looks at, on your Mac only

  • Which app is in front, so that it can show the buttons you set up for that app.
  • Where the pointer is, so that auto-hide knows when to hide and show the bar.
  • The contents of a folder, when you click a folder button to browse it.
  • Files you choose yourself, when you export or import a layout. A layout file never contains your licence key.
  • The clipboard, only when you press a paste text button: it temporarily holds your text so it can be pasted, then puts back what was there before.

None of this leaves your Mac.

Buttons that use the internet or your network

A few optional buttons contact other services, and only when you add them to your bar:

  • Stock price. BarDeck asks Yahoo Finance for a delayed quote for the symbol you typed (for example AAPL or ^FTSE), about every five minutes. Like any web request, it is visible to Yahoo as an ordinary request, including your IP address and the symbol. Nothing else about you or your Mac is sent.
  • Now Playing. For Apple Music and Spotify, BarDeck asks the app on your Mac what is playing. Spotify cover art is fetched from Spotify’s image servers, and Qobuz cover art from Qobuz’s image servers, with an ordinary request that includes your IP address and the cover’s name.
  • Sonos. BarDeck looks for Sonos speakers on your own network and reads what is playing from the one you choose. This stays on your local network and does not go to the internet (except the cover art, if the speaker is playing from a service that provides it).

None of these buttons sends your listening, your buttons or any personal information to me.

Permissions

If you use the press keys or paste text buttons, macOS will ask you to allow Accessibility access. The same permission lets the Qobuz Now Playing button read the track shown in the Qobuz app’s window. The New Folder button asks to control Finder (Automation), and the Music and Spotify buttons ask to control those apps; they only act if the app is already open. The Sonos button asks to find devices on your local network. BarDeck uses these only to carry out the button you pressed or to show the information you asked for.

Updates

BarDeck checks for new versions by fetching a small file from this website. Like any web request, it is visible to the site’s host (this site is hosted by GitHub Pages) as an ordinary request, including your IP address and the app’s name and version. BarDeck sends no personal information and no list of your apps or settings.

Buying

BarDeck is not on sale at the moment. When it is, payments are handled by the payment provider I sell through, not by me. I can see the details of an order, such as your name and email address, so that I can send your licence key and answer support questions, and the provider’s own privacy policy covers how it handles your payment details.

What I do not collect

  • No account and no sign-in.
  • No analytics, usage tracking or advertising identifiers.
  • No third-party trackers or advertising code.
  • No selling or sharing of data. There is no data of yours for me to sell.

Deleting your data

Delete the app and the ~/.config/bardeck folder to remove everything it keeps on your Mac.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the updated version will be posted on this page with a new date above.
